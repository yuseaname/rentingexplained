Renting Explained is an independent education resource for renters. We break down leases, security deposits, tenant rights, and the real costs of renting into plain-language guides you can actually use.

## Why we exist

Most renters first meet the rules of renting at the worst possible moment — when a lease is already in front of them, a deposit is at risk, or a landlord has stopped responding. The information exists, but it is scattered across state statutes, housing-court websites, and lease clauses written for the other side.

We started Renting Explained to put that information in one place, written for the person renting the apartment — not the landlord, not the property manager, and not the lawyer. Every guide assumes you have never done this before and explains each step from there.

## What we cover

Our guides follow the full renting lifecycle:

- **Finding a rental and applying** — what landlords look for, how income and credit are checked, and how to spot scams.
- **Leases** — what each clause means, what to negotiate, and what to refuse.
- **Security deposits** — what a landlord can charge, what they can deduct, and how to get the rest back.
- **Living in the unit** — repairs, landlord entry, rent increases, and your right to a habitable home.
- **Moving out** — notice timelines, move-out checklists, and disputes.

We focus on US renters because landlord-tenant law in the United States is set at the state level — and often at the city level on top of that. We always tell you when a rule depends on your jurisdiction so you know where to look next. See our [State Laws](/state-laws/) reference for a starting point.

## Our editorial standards

We research every guide against primary sources — state statutes, city housing departments, court self-help centers, and federal agencies like HUD and the CFPB. When the law varies by location, we say so instead of inventing a single answer. You can read the full standards in our [Editorial Policy](/editorial-policy).

**Important:** Our content is general educational information, not legal advice. It does not create an attorney-client relationship. For your specific situation, consult a qualified attorney or local tenant-rights organization in your jurisdiction.

## How we make money

Renting Explained is free to read. We are funded by advertising (including Google AdSense) and, where relevant, affiliate links to services renters actually use. Advertising and affiliate relationships never determine what we write or how we rate something. Full details are on our [Disclosure](/disclosure) page.

## Who is behind the site

Renting Explained is published by a small, independent editorial team in the United States. We are renters and former renters who have dealt with the same lease clauses, deposit disputes, and repair requests we write about.
