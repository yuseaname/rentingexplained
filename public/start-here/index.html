Your first apartment, step by step

Renting for the first time can feel like everyone else already knows the rules. This hub gathers everything you need in the order you actually need it.

The five steps

1. Figure out what you can afford

The 30% rule is a starting point, but the real cost of renting includes deposits, utilities, insurance, and moving expenses. Use our Rent Budget Calculator to see the full picture.

Read: How Much Rent Can I Afford? →

2. Gather your documents

Landlords usually want ID, proof of income, bank statements, and references. If you are self-employed or have no rental history, you will need a few extras.

Read: Proof of Income for Apartments →

3. Search and tour smart

Know where to look, what questions to ask, and which red flags mean you should walk away. Rental scams are common because renters are often in a hurry.

Read: Apartment Tour Checklist →
Read: How to Avoid Rental Scams →

4. Read the lease like a pro

A lease is a contract. Know what each clause means, what you can negotiate, and which terms should make you pause before signing.

Read: Understanding a Lease Agreement →

5. Document and move in

Protect your security deposit by photographing the unit at move-in, keeping receipts, and knowing how to report problems in writing.

Read: Apartment Move-In Checklist →

What to do after move-in

Once you’re in, your priorities shift to protecting your rights, your deposit, and your living situation:

  • Renter’s Rights: What Your Landlord Cannot Legally Do
  • Landlord Entry Notice Requirements

Key takeaways

  • Laws and fees vary by location — always check your state and city rules.
  • Read the lease carefully before signing; document everything at move-in.
  • Keep communication with your landlord in writing whenever possible.
