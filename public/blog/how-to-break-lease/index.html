Legal Ways Out (No Penalty)

1. Active Military Service

Servicemembers Civil Relief Act (SCRA):

  • Applies to active duty military
  • Permanent Change of Station (PCS) orders required
  • 30 days written notice
  • No penalty, return of deposit

What to provide:

  • Copy of PCS orders
  • Written notice
  • Date of departure

2. Domestic Violence

Protected in many states:

  • Provide documentation (police report, protective order)
  • Written notice
  • Usually 30 days notice required
  • No penalty

States with specific protections: CA, NY, TX, FL, IL, WA, OR, and others

3. Uninhabitable Unit

If landlord fails to maintain:

  • Document all issues
  • Give written notice to repair
  • Wait required time
  • If not fixed, constructively evicted

What counts:

  • No heat/hot water
  • Major plumbing failure
  • Mold infestation
  • Pest infestation
  • Structural dangers

4. Landlord Violated Lease

Examples:

  • Entered without notice repeatedly
  • Didn’t make agreed repairs
  • Harassed you
  • Changed terms unilaterally

Process:

  • Document violations
  • Written notice of breach
  • Opportunity to cure
  • Then you can terminate

Forfeit depositIf deposit is less than rent owed

Negotiate: Landlords may accept less than lease states

Strategy 3: Help Landlord Re-Rent

What to OfferWhy It Helps
Pay for advertisingReduces their cost
Allow showings while you live thereFills vacancy faster
Flexible move-out dateGives them options

Strategy 4: Mutual Agreement

Sometimes landlords want you out too:

  • They want to renovate
  • They want to raise rent significantly
  • They want to sell
  • They want to move in

If so: Negotiate cash for keys or penalty-free exit

How to Prevent Credit Damage

  1. Communicate in writing — Document everything
  2. Pay what you owe — Even if breaking lease
  3. Get agreement in writing — “Paid in full” statement
  4. Check credit report — Monitor for collections
  5. Dispute errors — If anything incorrect appears

Step-by-Step: Breaking a Lease Properly

Step 1: Review Your Lease

  • Find early termination clause
  • Check notice requirements
  • Identify any penalties

Step 2: Check State Law

  • Protected reasons in your state?
  • Required notice periods?
  • Landlord’s duty to mitigate?

Step 3: Document Your Reason

  • PCS orders (military)
  • Police report (domestic violence)
  • Photos (uninhabitable)
  • Communication records (landlord breach)

Step 4: Give Written Notice

[Date]

Dear [Landlord],

I am terminating my lease at [address] effective [date].

Reason: [legal reason OR "pursuant to early termination clause"]

[If applicable: Enclosed is documentation supporting this termination.]

Please provide:
1. Confirmation of termination
2. Final accounting of security deposit
3. Forwarding address for deposit return

Sincerely,
[Your name]

Step 5: Document Move-Out

  • Take photos of clean, empty unit
  • Return all keys
  • Get signed receipt for keys
  • Provide forwarding address

Step 6: Follow Up on Deposit

  • Wait until state deadline
  • If not returned, send demand letter
  • File in small claims if necessary

Frequently Asked Questions

Will breaking a lease show on my credit report?

Only if:

  • You don’t pay what you owe
  • Landlord sends to collections
  • Landlord sues and gets judgment

The act of breaking a lease itself does NOT appear on credit reports.

Can I break my lease if I lost my job?

Legally: No (in most states) Practically: Talk to your landlord. They may work with you.

What if my landlord refuses to let me out?

Options:

  • Find a replacement tenant
  • Pay termination fee
  • Sublet (if allowed)
  • Negotiate

Can I break a lease for safety concerns?

Yes, if:

  • Documented threats
  • Police reports
  • Restraining orders
  • Landlord failed to address security issues

How much notice do I have to give?

SituationTypical Notice
Early termination clausePer lease (usually 30-60 days)
Military30 days
Domestic violenceVaries (often 30 days)
Mutual agreementWhatever you negotiate

Quick Reference

Before breaking lease:

  • Read lease for termination clause
  • Check state law for protected reasons
  • Document everything
  • Calculate true cost of each option

Best options (in order):

  1. Protected legal reason (no penalty)
  2. Negotiate with landlord
  3. Find replacement tenant
  4. Pay termination fee
  5. Pay until re-rented

Never:

  • Just leave without notice
  • Stop paying rent without legal basis
  • Ignore communications from landlord

Breaking a lease isn’t ideal, but it doesn’t have to be a disaster. Do it right, protect yourself, and move on cleanly.

Key takeaways

  • Laws and fees vary by location — always check your state and city rules.
  • Read the lease carefully before signing; document everything at move-in.
  • Keep communication with your landlord in writing whenever possible.
