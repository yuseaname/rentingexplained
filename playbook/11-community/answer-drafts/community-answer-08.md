## Answer body (290 words)

Illinois is one of the strongest states for tenant habitability protections. Here's your playbook.

**Step 1 — Send a written repair request (today).**
Even if you've asked verbally, send a written request via email + certified mail. Include:
- The exact issue (no heat since [date])
- The habitability standard (in IL, heating must keep unit ≥ 68°F during the day, ≥ 60°F at night — 765 ILCS 105/5)
- A reasonable deadline (5–7 days for non-emergency, 24 hours for heat in winter)
- Your intention to "repair and deduct" or "withhold rent" if not addressed (cite the Illinois Code)

**Step 2 — Document everything.**
- Photos and videos of the issue (cold thermometer, ice on windows, etc.)
- Save all communications with the landlord.
- Note any health impacts (children, elderly, illness).

**Step 3 — If the landlord still doesn't act, you have options in Illinois:**

1. **Repair and deduct:** Pay a licensed contractor up to $500 or half a month's rent (whichever is greater) and deduct from next month's rent. **You must give proper notice first.**
2. **Withhold rent:** Put rent in an escrow account (NOT your landlord's account) and notify the landlord in writing. Spend it on alternative housing if needed.
3. **Contact the local building inspector:** Most cities have a code enforcement hotline. The inspector will send a notice to the landlord.
4. **Terminate the lease:** If the uninhabitable condition is serious and the landlord refuses to fix, you can break the lease without penalty (765 ILCS 740/5).

**Step 4 — Get free legal help.**
- **Illinois Tenant Union** (chicagotenant.org) — free advice and representation.
- **Legal Aid Chicago** (legalaidchicago.org) — free attorneys for low-income tenants.
- **Cook County Legal Aid Hotline** — (312) 341-1070.

Full breakdown of repair rights in all 50 states, sample letters, and a repair-and-deduct calculator: https://rentingexplained.com/blog/tenant-rights-when-landlord-won-t-make-repairs/
