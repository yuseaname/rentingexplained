## Answer body (290 words)

This is one of the most common deposit disputes — landlords use vague "cleaning and damages" to justify large deductions. You have more leverage than you think.

**Step 1 — Demand an itemized breakdown with receipts.**
Most states require landlords to provide an **itemized list of deductions** with the actual or estimated cost of each item. "Cleaning and damages" without specifics violates the law in CA, NY, TX, FL, IL, and most others.

Send a written demand (email + certified mail) requiring:
- Itemized list of every deduction
- Receipts or estimated costs for each
- Photos of the alleged damage

**Step 2 — Dispute in writing.**
Cite your state's deposit statute and your move-in documentation. If you have move-in photos showing the unit was already in that condition, that's your smoking gun.

**Step 3 — Negotiate or escalate.**
Most landlords will refund at least part of the disputed amount when faced with a written demand citing state law. If not:

- **Small claims court:** most states allow you to sue for the deposit + 2–3× damages for bad-faith withholding (CA: §1950.5(l), NY: GOL §234, IL: 765 ILCS 710).
- **State attorney general complaint:** every state AG handles landlord-tenant issues and many will send a letter that resolves the dispute.
- **Local tenant union or legal aid:** free attorneys in most cities.

**Key leverage:** The burden of proof shifts to the landlord once they fail to return the deposit on time or fail to provide a proper itemization.

If you're in CA, NY, TX, FL, or IL — full statute citations and a sample dispute letter: https://rentingexplained.com/blog/how-to-get-security-deposit-back/
