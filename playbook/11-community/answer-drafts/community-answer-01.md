## Answer body (260 words)

Texas Property Code §92.103 gives landlords **30 days from move-out** to either return the deposit or send an itemized list of deductions. You're past that window.

**Step 1 — Send a written demand letter today.**
Include:
- Your forwarding address (they legally cannot withhold for not having this).
- The move-out date.
- A demand for the full deposit.
- A reference to §92.103.
- A deadline of 7 days.

Send by **certified mail** AND email. Keep both confirmations.

**Step 2 — Escalate if no response.**
Texas allows you to sue for **the deposit + $100 + attorney's fees** if they wrongfully withhold in bad faith (after the 30-day deadline, the burden shifts to them).

Small claims court in Texas handles disputes up to $20,000, no lawyer required. Filing fee is usually under $50.

**Step 3 — Document everything.**
- Your move-in photos (timestamped).
- All communications with the landlord.
- The original lease.
- Your move-out notice.

**Common landlord excuses that won't hold up:**
- "We didn't have your forwarding address" — not a valid defense if you gave it in writing.
- "We needed to clean" — must be itemized, and "cleaning" alone isn't enough.
- "We'll get to it" — the deadline is the deadline.

If they send an itemized list after the 30 days, you can still dispute deductions that aren't reasonable wear-and-tear.
