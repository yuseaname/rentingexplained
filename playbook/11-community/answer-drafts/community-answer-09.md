## Answer body (260 words)

Most states require **24–48 hours written notice** before landlord entry, except for genuine emergencies (fire, flood, gas leak). What you're describing is almost certainly illegal.

**What to do:**

**Step 1 — Document every entry.**
- Date and time
- Whether you were given notice
- What was said
- Photos if anything was moved

Save texts, emails, security camera footage. If your building has a fob entry log, request it (FREQ or state public records law applies in some states).

**Step 2 — Send a written cease-and-desist letter.**
Cite your state's entry-notice law. Common statutes:
- **California:** 24 hours written notice (CC §1954)
- **New York:** Reasonable notice (typically 24 hours) (RPL §235)
- **Texas:** 24 hours written notice (Texas Property Code §92.009)
- **Florida:** 24 hours written notice, except in emergencies (FL Stat §83.59)
- **Illinois:** 24 hours written notice (Chicago RLTO or IL Compiled Statutes)

**Step 3 — Send via email + certified mail.**
Keep both confirmations. The letter should:
- Reference the prior unannounced entries
- Cite the statute
- Demand written notice for all future entries
- Reserve your rights to sue for trespass

**Step 4 — Escalate if it continues.**
- File a police report for trespass (rare but powerful).
- Sue in small claims court for damages (most states allow $1,000–$10,000).
- Contact a tenant union or legal aid society.

**Practical tip:** Get a $30 doorbell camera or a wyze cam inside. The footage is your evidence.

Full breakdown of entry-notice laws in all 50 states, sample cease-and-desist letter, and small claims process: https://rentingexplained.com/blog/renters-rights-guide/
