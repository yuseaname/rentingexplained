## Answer body (290 words)

Congrats! Here's a 7-point checklist that will save you thousands in your first year.

**1. Budget 3–4× monthly rent in cash before signing.**
Most first-timers forget that the deposit, first/last month, and application fees stack up. Realistic budget: $4,000–$8,000 cash on hand for a $1,500/mo apartment.

**2. The 5 documents your landlord can legally ask for:**
- Photo ID
- Pay stubs (most recent 2)
- Bank statements
- References (previous landlord + 1 personal)
- Proof of renter's insurance

Anything else is a red flag.

**3. Read Sections 8 and 9 of the lease.**
Those cover defaults and maintenance. If they say "in a timely manner," negotiate "48 hours to respond, 14 days to complete habitability repairs."

**4. Document the unit on move-in day.**
Photograph every wall, scratch, stain, fixture. Timestamp on. Email the photos to yourself. This is the single biggest determinant of whether you get your deposit back.

**5. Set up renter's insurance BEFORE picking up keys.**
Most policies bind in 5 minutes, cost $15–$25/month, and protect you from liability and loss.

**6. Within 48 hours of move-in:**
- Forward mail via USPS
- Put utilities in your name
- Change locks (with permission)
- Test every outlet, faucet, appliance, smoke detector

**7. Three clauses to never sign as-written:**
- "Landlord may enter at any time" → ask for 24–48 hour notice
- "Tenant responsible for all repairs under $500" → habitability is the landlord's job
- "Security deposit non-refundable" → illegal in most states

**Hidden costs to plan for:**
- Application fees ($30–$75 per property)
- Pet fees (one-time + monthly)
- Parking ($50–$200/mo)
- Storage ($50–$150/mo)
- Utilities in older buildings ($200+)

Full 30-page guide with templates, scripts, and state-specific rules: https://rentingexplained.com/blog/first-time-renters-guide/
