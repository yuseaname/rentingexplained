## Answer body (240 words)

The 30% rule is outdated — it was invented in 1969 for public housing and doesn't account for modern taxes, student debt, or childcare.

**Quick math for $55k:**

- **Gross monthly:** $4,583
- **Net (after taxes, ~22% effective):** $3,575
- **25% of net:** $894
- **30% of gross:** $1,375

Those two numbers are $500 apart. That difference is the line between saving and paycheck-to-paycheck.

**The 2026 rule:**
- **25% of net** is your rent ceiling.
- **35% of net** is your all-in housing ceiling (rent + utilities + insurance + parking).
- If all-in housing exceeds **40% of net**, the apartment is unaffordable.

**For your $55k salary, that means:**
- Sustainable rent: **$850–$1,100/mo** depending on your state taxes.
- All-in housing cap: **$1,250/mo**.

**Three numbers to budget for:**
- Take-home pay (after tax AND pre-tax benefits like 401k, health insurance)
- Fixed debt payments (student loans, car, etc.)
- Savings goal (10–15% of net is healthy)

**The formula:**
> Net income × 0.25 = rent ceiling

If you have high student debt or childcare, drop it to 0.20.

Full breakdown with 4 budget scenarios (single earner, dual income, high debt, high savings) and a free spreadsheet: https://rentingexplained.com/blog/how-much-rent-can-i-afford/
