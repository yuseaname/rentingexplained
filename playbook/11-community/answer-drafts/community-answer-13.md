## Answer body (260 words)

If you're both on the lease, the landlord doesn't care who pays — they care that rent arrives. If your roommate is short, **you pay the full amount and pursue your roommate separately for their share**. Missing rent hits your credit and rental history, not theirs.

**Step 1 — Document everything now.**
- Texts/emails showing they owe you
- Venmo/PayPal records
- The lease (whose name is on it?)
- Any written roommate agreement

**Step 2 — Talk to them directly.**
Most roommate disputes are solvable with a calm conversation. Use the "I" framework:
- "I'm worried about my credit."
- "I'd like to set up auto-pay for everyone's share."
- "If we can't fix this, I need to consider other options."

**Step 3 — If they're on the lease, use the formal process.**
- Send a written demand for their share.
- If they refuse, you can sue in small claims court (most states allow $5K–$10K).
- Or, ask the landlord to remove them from the lease (this requires their cooperation and a new applicant).

**Step 4 — If they're NOT on the lease (sublet), you have more leverage.**
- Give them written notice to pay or vacate (typically 3–14 days).
- File for eviction if they don't leave.
- Sue for unpaid rent in small claims.

**Step 5 — Protect yourself going forward.**
- Get a roommate agreement in writing (free template: https://rentingexplained.com/blog/roommate-agreement-template/).
- Set up auto-pay for rent.
- Pay the full rent yourself and bill your roommate separately.
- Consider separate leases (when possible) so one roommate's nonpayment doesn't affect your credit.

**The hard truth:** If they're consistently late, they will be late again. Decide now whether to (a) work it out, (b) ask for removal from the lease, or (c) end the lease and move.

Full breakdown of the legal options, sample demand letter, and roommate agreement template: https://rentingexplained.com/blog/roommate-agreement-template/
