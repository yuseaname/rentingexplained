## Answer body (260 words)

In California, **yes and no** — it depends on how long you lived there and what kind of paint damage occurred.

Under CA Civil Code §1950.5, "normal wear and tear" cannot be deducted from a security deposit. The Department of Consumer Affairs has clarified what counts as normal:

**Generally NOT deductible:**
- Faded paint
- Small nail holes (under ¼ inch)
- Minor scuffs on door frames
- Worn paint from sun exposure over time

**Generally deductible:**
- Unapproved paint colors (if your lease required neutral colors)
- Large holes, gouges, or damage from removed wallpaper
- Paint ruined by smoking or pets
- Unauthorized murals

**The 3-year rule:**
California courts have repeatedly held that paint older than **3 years** is generally considered normal wear and tear, even if there are visible marks. So if you've lived there 4+ years and they want to repaint, they're usually eating that cost.

**What to do if they deduct for painting:**

1. Ask for the itemized list with photos (they're required to provide this).
2. If they can't justify the deduction, dispute in writing within the 21-day window.
3. If they refuse, you can sue in small claims for **up to 2× the deposit** for bad-faith withholding (CA Civil Code §1950.5(l)).

**Tip:** Always photograph the unit at move-in with timestamps on. It's the single biggest determinant of who wins these disputes.

Full breakdown of what's deductible vs. not in CA, plus a sample dispute letter: https://rentingexplained.com/blog/how-to-get-security-deposit-back/
