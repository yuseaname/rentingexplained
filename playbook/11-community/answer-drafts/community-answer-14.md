## Answer body (300 words)

**Yes. This is almost certainly a scam.** Here are the red flags you're seeing:

🚩 **Out-of-state landlord** — common in scams. Real landlords live locally or have a local property manager.

🚩 **Won't let you view the unit in person** — scammers often can't show the unit because they don't own it.

🚩 **Pushes for an upfront deposit before you sign a lease** — legitimate landlords wait for the deposit after the lease is signed.

🚩 **Uses Zillow/trulia-photos copied from other listings** — reverse image search the photos. If they appear on other listings in different cities, it's a scam.

🚩 **Asks for payment via wire transfer, gift card, or cryptocurrency** — every legitimate payment is traceable. Wire transfers and gift cards are untraceable.

🚩 **Creates urgency** — "act now, someone else is interested" is a classic pressure tactic.

**What to do:**

1. **Don't send money.** No matter how good the deal is.
2. **Reverse image search** the photos (Google Images, TinEye).
3. **Check the county assessor's office** — confirm the landlord owns the property.
4. **Verify the listing** — if the apartment is also on Zillow, contact the Zillow listing agent, not the one who emailed you.
5. **Search the landlord's name + "scam"** in Google.
6. **Check the address** on Google Maps — if it's a parking lot, vacant lot, or doesn't exist, it's a scam.

**If you've already sent money:**
- Call your bank immediately to attempt a chargeback.
- File a report with the FTC (reportfraud.ftc.gov).
- File a police report.
- File a complaint with the IC3 (ic3.gov) if it crossed state lines.

**Real-world stat:** The FTC received 11,000+ rental scam reports in 2024, with median losses of $1,500. The category is growing 30% year-over-year.

Full breakdown of 12 rental scam red flags, the verification workflow, and a sample reverse-image-search script: https://rentingexplained.com/blog/rental-scams-how-to-avoid/
