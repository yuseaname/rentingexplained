## Answer body (260 words)

Both can work. The math usually favors a roommate if your city is HCOL (NYC, SF, Boston, LA, Seattle, DC). Here's the framework:

**Roommate math:**
- 2BR with roommate: $1,400–$1,800 each in most HCOL cities
- 1BR alone: $2,200–$2,800
- **Savings: $800–$1,000/mo** with a roommate

**Move-further-out math:**
- 30 min commute: 20–30% lower rent
- 45 min commute: 30–40% lower rent
- **Hidden cost:** gas, transit, parking, time (5–10 hrs/week extra)

**Quick decision tree:**

1. **If you make less than $60k and live in a HCOL city:** roommate is almost always the better deal unless you have a free car and value solo time.
2. **If you can work from home:** move further out — save $500–$1,000/mo without losing commute time.
3. **If you have pets or need quiet for sleep/work:** roommate is risky; move further out or negotiate a small studio.
4. **If you're saving for a home down payment:** the highest savings rate wins. That's almost always roommate.

**Roommate pro tip:** Put EVERYTHING in a roommate agreement — rent split, utility split, guest policy, quiet hours, food/shared items, move-out terms. Free template: https://rentingexplained.com/blog/roommate-agreement-template/

**Move-further-out pro tip:** Test the commute twice — once at peak hour (7–9am), once at off-peak. A "20-minute" drive can become 50 minutes in real traffic.

For most people in HCOL cities, 12–24 months with a roommate while building savings is the highest-leverage move.
